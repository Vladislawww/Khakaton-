# Getting started with Lumora

Lumora Learning is an online tutoring service for children. Every lesson is live and one-to-one: one tutor, one child, on a shared interactive whiteboard in the browser. Lumora teaches math from kindergarten to grade 12 and reading and writing from kindergarten to grade 10.

## What Lumora is and who it is for
Lumora matches each child with one patient tutor and keeps the same tutor every week, so trust and momentum build. Lessons adapt to how the child learns.

Lumora works well for three kinds of families:
- **Kids who feel stuck.** The tutor finds the exact gap behind the frustration, fills it calmly, and only then moves on.
- **Parents short on time.** Lessons fit around after-school activities and dinner. Parents get a short recap after each lesson instead of another evening of homework battles.
- **Learners racing ahead.** Tutors add stretch problems, competition practice and reading lists for curious kids who are bored in class.

Key numbers: 4.8 out of 5 average rating from about 2,300 parent ratings, 94% of families stay past month three, the first lesson usually happens within 48 hours, and fewer than 1 in 9 tutor applicants are hired.

## How to start: three steps
1. **Book a free trial lesson.** Click "Book a free lesson" anywhere on the site, or pick your child's grade on the home page. The first lesson is free and no card is needed.
2. **Free skills check.** A friendly, game-like check of about 20 minutes (15 minutes for grade 1) shows what your child already knows and where the gaps are. There are no grades and no pressure. It covers math, reading, or both, and results are shared with you the same day.
3. **Tutor match and plan.** Within 48 hours Lumora matches a tutor based on grade, subject, schedule and personality notes from the skills check. The tutor turns the results into a week-by-week plan that follows your state's standards and your child's interests. The plan is adjusted every month.

## What happens after the free lesson
After the free trial lesson, the tutor shares a recommended plan and pace (for example, one, two or three lessons a week). To keep the same tutor and the same time slot, choose a subscription plan on the pricing page. If you don't choose a plan, nothing is charged and nothing renews.

## Grades and subjects covered
- **Math:** kindergarten through grade 12. From counting and place value to algebra, geometry, trigonometry, pre-calculus, calculus, statistics and test preparation.
- **Reading and writing:** kindergarten through grade 10. Phonics, fluency, comprehension, vocabulary, spelling, creative writing and essay writing.
- Families can take one subject or bundle math and reading together, which lowers the price per lesson.

## Do you teach other subjects, like science, languages or music?
No. Lumora teaches only two subjects: math, and reading and writing. There are no lessons in science (chemistry, physics, biology), foreign languages, music or piano, art, coding, or test prep outside math and reading.

## Is the curriculum aligned with school standards?
Yes. Every plan follows Common Core and individual state standards, so school and tutoring point the same way. Tutors can also work directly on school homework: upload the worksheet in the dashboard and the tutor folds it into the lesson.

## How long is a lesson?
A standard lesson is 50 minutes. Kindergarten and grade 1 lessons are 30 minutes, because that fits a young child's attention span. Younger children in other grades can also choose 30-minute lessons instead of 50.

## What does my child need for a lesson?
- A laptop or tablet with a camera (Windows or Mac laptops, Chromebooks, or iPads from the last five years).
- Headphones.
- A quiet corner.
- An up-to-date Chrome, Edge or Safari browser. Lessons run in the browser, so there is nothing to install.
Tablets with a stylus are great for drawing on the whiteboard. A parent is welcome to sit in, especially with younger children.

## Is my child too young for online lessons?
No. Lessons for the youngest children are short (30 minutes), full of games and movement breaks, and a parent can sit in. Most families with a first grader choose two lessons a week.
