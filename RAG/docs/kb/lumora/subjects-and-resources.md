# Subjects, grades and free resources

What children learn at Lumora by subject and grade, plus free worksheets and the blog.

## Math tutoring by grade
- **Elementary (K–5):** counting, place value, the four operations, first fractions, number sense, built with games and lots of encouragement.
- **Middle school (6–8):** ratios, negative numbers, equations and the jump to pre-algebra.
- **High school (9–12):** algebra I and II, geometry, trigonometry, pre-calculus, calculus, statistics and exam preparation, with a tutor who helps plan the whole semester.
Most families see clearer understanding within four to six weeks of consistent 1:1 lessons.

- Tools: open_link

## Grade 1 math
First-grade lessons are 30 minutes, playful and full of movement. Topics: counting and writing numbers to 120, adding and subtracting within 20, tens and ones (place value), measuring length and telling time, 2D and 3D shapes. There is a free 15-minute grade 1 math check. Signs a first grader may need help: counts on fingers for every sum, mixes up "more" and "less", gets upset during math homework, struggles to count past 50, finds word problems confusing, or the teacher has mentioned math concerns.
- URL: /grade-1-math.html
- Tools: open_link, book_free_lesson

## Reading and writing tutoring by grade
- Kindergarten: letters and sounds. Grade 1: blending and fluency. Grade 2: chapter books. Grade 3: comprehension. Grade 4: vocabulary. Grade 5: analysis. Grades 6–8: essays and literature. Grades 9–10: critical reading.
- Reading lessons use science-of-reading methods and a book list built around the child's favorite topics. Each week: read aloud, discuss, write and review new words.
- Dyslexia-friendly: structured, multisensory lessons at a gentle pace.
- Writing: from first sentences to five-paragraph essays.
Most kids add a full reading level within one school term.

- Tools: open_link

## How do I know if my child is behind in reading?
Signs include guessing words, avoiding reading aloud, and trouble retelling a story. The free reading check (we listen to your child read and measure fluency and understanding) will tell you for sure.
